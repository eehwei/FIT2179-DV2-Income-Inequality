Section 1: The Wealth Gap

Where is the Money in Malaysia?

Malaysia's economy is deeply divided. Selangor and Kuala Lumpur earn over RM10,000 monthly, while Kelantan and Perlis struggle below RM5,000. This map reveals stark regional disparities.

Wealth clusters around the Klang Valley; rural and East Malaysian states lag behind. Geography determines opportunity.

The Ranking

Putrajaya earns RM7,000+ more than Kelantan. This bar chart ranks all states.

Clear pattern: federal territories at top, rural states at bottom.

Section 2: Income Categories in Malaysia

Understanding B40, M40, and T20

Malaysia divides households into three income tiers:

B40 (RM5,250/month): poorest 40% | M40 (RM5,251-11,819): middle class | T20 (RM11,820+): wealthiest 20%. These categories help target assistance programs.

Demographics of Prosperity

Does income level match age structure? B40 states have more dependents; T20 states have more workers. This affects economic health.

Economic prosperity requires enough productive workers to support dependents.

Spending Priorities: Survival vs Investment

Where does money go?

B40: 60% on survival (food, housing), only 8% on education and savings. T20: 35% on future investment. This is why poverty is a trap—survival costs leave no room to invest.

How They Earn: Sources of Income

It's not just how much you earn—it's where money comes from.

B40: 64% from wages + 16.5% from gigs. T20: 25.5% from investments (passive income). The divide: B40 trades time for money; T20 owns assets that generate income.

Section 3: Historical Trends

Five Decades of Growth—But Not for Everyone

The rich-poor gap, established in the 1970s, has barely changed for 50 years. Economic crises created dips, but relative positions stayed the same.

Every State's Story

Selangor and Kuala Lumpur show steep growth; Kelantan and Perlis show stagnation. West Malaysian states grew faster; East Malaysian states grew modestly.

Section 4: Poverty Reality

Who's Struggling to Survive?

States in red: >10% poverty. States in green: <3% poverty. Urban-rural contrast is stark.

The Income-Poverty Connection

Higher income = lower poverty. But the relationship isn't perfect—some states defy the trend.

Bottom-right (Selangor): high income, low poverty. Top-left (Kelantan, Sabah): low income, high poverty. Development requires lifting the poorest households, not just raising averages.

Section 5: Inequality Within

Measuring the Gap

The Gini coefficient (0 = perfect equality, 1 = one person has all) shows Sabah and Kelantan have the widest rich-poor gaps. Pahang and Terengganu are more equal—but equally modest.

Half a Century of Inequality

Inequality has darkened over time. Sabah and Kelantan show persistent deep red. Economic growth hasn't translated to equal distribution.
